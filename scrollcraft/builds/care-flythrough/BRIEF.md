# The Care: a walk through the house

Interviewed 2026-09-24, in session. The answers below are TJ's words where he
gave them, and his choices from the four questions asked before building.

## The answers

**What the page is for.** "In the care page, it will show what does our
service offer: taking care of the baby for you, treatments, it will cook for
you, will do the day to day household chore for you."

**The asset.** One film, `full flythrough v2.mp4`, 26.9 seconds, continuous,
"no jump cut, fade transition". The camera enters the house, goes to the
kitchen where the carer is cooking, pans left to the carer ironing, pans up to
the carer holding the baby, pans right to the carer giving the mother a belly
treatment. It then flies out through the bedroom window into the sky and
fades to near white.

**The mechanic.** "The scroll is sort of the remote control of this. When you
scroll down, it will forward the video, and then as you go scroll further it
will show each scene."

**The feeling.** "A 3D effect where you scroll it looks like the visitor takes
you in to the house, show you what your service does." "Show the service in a
creative way."

**One unbroken world, or distinct scenes?** One unbroken world. This is the
case the skill allows a continuous chain for: the brief is literally one
continuous journey and the footage already exists.

**Choices made before building.**
- Copy: short, one headline and one line per room, drafted to the Brand Pack
  voice for TJ to edit.
- Ending: resolve on a closing line with Enquire, then a paper section
  recapping the four services, then the footer.
- Phone: full screen, the crop follows the carer room by room.
- Depth: words fixed in the room, pointer depth on desktop, floor plan guide.
  A doorway card (the house framed on paper, opening to full bleed) was built
  and then dropped at TJ's review the same day: "I want that house full
  screen at the beginning. There's just like a text, scroll and come in."

## The feeling curve

| Room | Feeling | What on screen causes it |
|---|---|---|
| Outside | Invited | The house fills the screen with one line, "Scroll and come in."; the first scroll walks you to the door |
| Kitchen | Nourished | The camera settles on her cooking; the words arrive as you do |
| Living room | Let off | You are asleep on the sofa and the house is still being kept |
| Nursery | **Peak.** Tender | The only vertical move in the film: the camera rises through the ceiling and finds your baby held |
| Bedroom | Cared for | The attention finally turns to you |
| Sky | Resolved | The camera leaves through the window, the sky lifts into the paper, one line holds |

**The peak.** "You look up and she is upstairs holding the baby." The nursery
has the largest share of the scroll (1.4 of 8.7 viewport heights, tied with
the kitchen, after the quickest move on the page).

**It's the site where** you walk through your own house and watch someone
look after all of it.

**Authored silence.** None. The film advances on every step of the scroll;
the rooms settle by slowing, never by stopping.

## Signature move

The words are placed in the room. Each room's lines drift the way the room
does when the camera turns (a pan left carries them right, the rise into the
nursery brings them down from above), while a hairline floor plan of the
house in section tracks the route and lets you fly to any room.
